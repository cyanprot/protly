# Keyboard and app UI and UX audit (2026-10-30)

A research pass over the iPhone keyboard (`ios-app/Sources/ProtlyKeyboard`) and the container app
(`ios-app/Sources/Protly/ProtlyApp.swift`) as they are on `main` at 54d94c1. No code was changed.

## Sources and limits

- Code: every file in `ios-app/Sources/ProtlyKeyboard` and `ios-app/Sources/Protly`, plus the parts of ProtlyCore
  the UI shows (`ActionAPI.swift` error messages, timeouts and `mode(forDraft:)`, `AboutMe.swift` panel text) and
  `Resources/keyboard-prompt.txt` (what the result panel's second line holds).
- Screenshots: `runs/e301e04-15/` on the `ci-shots` branch (iOS 26.5 simulator, iPhone 13 Pro, 390 pt wide, dark
  mode, English (US)). `01-apple.png` is Apple's keyboard; `02`-`07` and `c-*.png` are Protly. The accessibility
  trees in the same folder (`tree-01-apple.txt`, `tree-06-word-typed.txt`) give the exact hit frames quoted below.
- Apple's Human Interface Guidelines, read on 2026-10-30 through Apple's documentation data endpoint
  (`developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/<page>.json`; the HTML pages render
  in JavaScript only): "Virtual keyboards" (custom keyboards section) and "Accessibility". Quotes are from those
  pages.
- Gboard, Grammarly Keyboard and Apple's Writing Tools are described from product knowledge, not checked
  again in this session. Their behaviour changes between releases, so treat those comparisons as direction, not
  spec.

What the screenshots do not cover: the panel (Translate, Polish, Read clipboard, errors, About me) never appears,
because the CI job has no API key and no panel scenario. Light mode, Dynamic Type sizes, landscape, VoiceOver
in use, and the real phone (haptics, sound, other host apps) are not shown either. Findings about those areas come
from the code and are marked "from code".

### HIG lines this audit leans on

- "Provide an obvious and easy way to switch between keyboards."
- "Avoid duplicating system-provided keyboard features." (the globe and dictation keys under the keyboard)
- "Consider providing a keyboard tutorial in your app. ... tell people how to choose your keyboard, activate it
  during text entry, use it, and switch back to the standard keyboard. Avoid displaying help content within the
  keyboard itself."
- "Play the standard keyboard sound while people type." (`playInputClick()`)
- Accessibility: iOS default control size 44x44 pt, minimum 28x28 pt. "Support larger text sizes ... Ideally,
  give people the option to enlarge text by at least 200 percent." "Describe your app's interface and content
  for VoiceOver."

## What the bar shows today

`ProtlyBar.swift` splits the bar into three equal thirds (lines 18-22), like Apple's predictive bar.

| Slot | Shows | When | Tap does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left | The other language's reading of the word in quotes (`“ㅗ디ㅣㅐ”` for `hello`, `“qhk”` for 봐) | While a word is typed, and after it until the next word or a non-space ends it (`ws-1`, `ws-2`, `ws-3`) | Flips that word to the other language |
| Center | `Undo` after an Insert; else `Translate` (any Hangul in the field) or `Polish` (no Hangul, or a `ko:` prefix); else `Read clipboard` if new text was copied and the field is empty | The action shows only when no word is being typed (`KeyboardViewController.swift:338-339`) | Opens the panel in place of the keys |
| Right | One fix or completion for the word being typed (`the` for `teh`, `안녕` for `안녀`) | While a word is typed; gone after a space or punctuation | Replaces the word, adds a space |
| Protly key (bottom row, Apple's emoji spot) | `character.bubble` glyph, always the same | Always | Whatever fits: the center's action, or Read clipboard on an empty field (`act(nil)`, line 210) |

Measured from the trees: Apple's three predictions are 130 x 44 pt each (`tree-01-apple.txt` lines 38-40).
Protly's left slot button is 73.7 x 20.3 pt and `Polish` is 45.7 x 20.3 pt (`tree-06-word-typed.txt` lines 40-41).

## Issues

Severity: **high** hurts every session or blocks a feature; **medium** causes mistakes or confusion often;
**low** is polish. IDs are for reference in later plans.

### Bar

**B1. Bar tap targets are the text, not the slot. High.**
- Where: `ProtlyBar.swift:19-21, 31, 39, 46-50`; `tree-06-word-typed.txt:40-41` vs `tree-01-apple.txt:38-40`.
- User sees: the slot looks like a third of the bar, but only the word itself takes the tap: 20.3 pt tall,
  45.7 pt wide for `Polish`. A tap in the empty part of the slot, or slightly above the word, does nothing.
- Why it hurts: below the HIG's 28 pt minimum, let alone the 44 pt default. Apple's slots are 130 x 44 pt, so
  people's thumbs are used to hitting anywhere in the third. Missed taps on the flip slot leave a wrong-language
  word in the field.
- Fix: make each slot the button. Put the `Text` inside the label with
  `.frame(maxWidth: .infinity, maxHeight: .infinity).contentShape(Rectangle())`, and let the hit area cover the
  whole band above row 1 (the 17.5 pt `topBand` plus the 29 pt row, about 46 pt), not only the text row.

**B2. Actions look exactly like word suggestions. High.**
- Where: `ProtlyBar.swift:46-50`; screenshots `06-word-typed.png`, `c-6.8-1.png`, `c-ws-2.png`, `c-8.5-1o.png`.
- User sees: `Polish` and `Translate` in the same font, color and place as a suggested word. On an English draft
  the center reads `Polish` after almost every space.
- Why it hurts: the center is where Apple puts its best prediction, so a quick tap there is a habit. Here that
  tap hides the keys, moves the cursor to the end of the field (B11) and sends the draft to the API. People
  cannot tell an action from a word before they tap. Gboard marks its tools with icons in a toolbar, Grammarly
  with its own badge and colored cards, Apple's Writing Tools with its own glyph.
- Fix: give actions a symbol and the accent tint, words stay plain: `translate` + "→ English",
  `wand.and.sparkles` + "Polish", `doc.on.clipboard` + "Read clipboard", `arrow.uturn.backward` + "Undo". A thin
  capsule behind the action also helps.

**B3. The center action flickers with every word. Medium.**
- Where: `KeyboardViewController.swift:338-339` (`session.isComposing ? nil`); `c-6.8-1-1-right.png` (typing
  `teh`: center empty) vs `c-6.8-1.png` (after the space: `Polish`); `c-8.5-8o.png` (three-paragraph draft, last
  word `ㅠㅠ` still composing: center empty).
- User sees: `Polish` or `Translate` appears at each space and vanishes at the next letter.
- Why it hurts: constant motion in the center of the bar while typing, and the one moment people want the action
  (right after the last letter) is the moment it is gone; they have to type a space first. `act()` already commits
  the word before it reads the field (line 211), so the gate protects nothing.
- Fix: decide `draftMode` from the field text plus the marked word and keep it shown while composing. If showing it
  mid-word feels noisy, keep the last action visible but dimmed instead of removing it.

**B4. The left slot shows nonsense almost all the time. Medium.**
- Where: `ProtlyBar.swift:29-33`; screenshots `06-word-typed.png` (`“ㅗ디ㅣㅐ”`), `c-6.8-3-1-right.png`
  (`“새ㅡㅐ”`), `c-6.8-2-1-right.png` (`“ㄱㄷ챾ㅍㄷ”`), `c-8.5-7o.png` (`“akfgowy”`), `c-8.5-1o.png`
  (`“tkfdkTek”`), `c-8.5-8o.png` (`“bb”`).
- User sees: a jamo or Latin string in quotes for nearly every word, including words the detector got right.
- Why it hurts: the most-seen element in the bar is noise, so people learn to ignore it, which defeats the
  flip when it matters. The quotes also borrow Apple's convention with the opposite meaning: in Apple's bar
  (`01-apple.png`, `“tomorrow”`) the quoted word is what you typed, and tapping it keeps it; here the quoted word
  is what you did not type, and tapping it replaces your word.
- Fix: show the alternative at full contrast only when it is plausible (a word-list hit in the other language,
  or a small detector margin); otherwise draw it in `.tertiary` or replace it with a short flip chip ("한" or
  "EN"). Drop the quotes and put a language marker in front instead ("한 안녕", "EN hello").

**B5. Translate and Polish do not say which language. Medium.**
- Where: `ProtlyBar.swift:48`; `c-8.5-1o.png` (`ru: ...` shows `Translate`), `c-8.5-7o.png` (`ko: ...` shows
  `Polish`), `c-8.5-9o.png` (a Spanish draft misread as Korean shows `Translate`; check 8.5-9o FAIL).
- User sees: the same two words whatever the prefix or the detected language.
- Why it hurts: the prefix feature (`ru:`, `ja:`, `ko:`) gives no sign it was understood, and a misread draft is
  only found after a paid call and a wait. `ActionAPI.mode(forDraft:)` sends any field with one Hangul character
  to Translate, so a stray `ㅋㅋ` in an English draft flips the action too.
- Fix: put the target in the label: "→ English", "→ Русский", "Polish (한국어)", from `Languages.prefix`. A
  long-press on the center could offer the other action (Polish an English draft that has `ㅋㅋ`, Translate
  anyway).

**B6. The bar is blank on an empty field. Medium.**
- Where: `ProtlyBar.swift:49-51`, `KeyboardViewController.swift:348-351`; `02-first-open.png`, `c-6.8-10.png`.
- User sees: an empty 52 pt band above the keys. Read clipboard appears only if the clipboard changed since
  Protly last looked.
- Why it hurts: nothing on the first open says Protly can translate a copied message, and someone who copied a
  message before opening the keyboard (the common case: copy in one app, switch, tap the field) sees nothing
  because `clipboardIsNew` was already cleared, or the first-run line 346 marked that copy as old.
- Fix: on an empty field, show Read clipboard whenever `UIPasteboard.hasStrings` is true (it does not trigger
  the Allow Paste prompt), dimmed when it is not new. This is a control, not help text, so it stays within the
  HIG's "avoid help content within the keyboard".

**B7. A missed fix cannot be taken later. Medium.**
- Where: `Words.swift:37-46` (the suggestion is only for the word being typed); `c-6.8-2-1-right.png`
  (`receive` offered) then `c-6.8-2.png` (after `.`, field `recieve.`, right slot empty).
- User sees: the right slot offers the fix while the word is typed; once the user types a space or a period, the
  fix is gone and the typo stays.
- Why it hurts: Protly never autocorrects (a choice the code states: "Only a tap puts it in"), so the right slot
  is the only correction path, and it closes at the moment most people look up from the keys. Apple corrects on
  the space and lets you undo it; Gboard and Grammarly underline the word so it can be fixed later.
- Fix: keep the last word's fix in the right slot after a space or punctuation until the next word starts, and
  let a tap replace that word in place (the session already tracks `lastTail` for the flip after a space). An
  optional "autocorrect on space" setting would match Apple for those who want it.

**B8. The bar does not look like Apple's. Low.**
- Where: `ProtlyBar.swift:18-26`, `ProtlyLayout.swift:10-13` (`toolbarHeight`, `topBand`); the bar band of `01-apple.png`
  next to `06-word-typed.png`.
- User sees: no dividers between slots; words in pure white (`.primary`) where Apple uses a softer, tinted label
  color; text centers 32 pt below the glass top against Apple's 22 pt, so the words sit 20 pt above row 1's keys
  against Apple's 30 pt (frames from the two trees).
- Why it hurts: the bar reads as a different, heavier strip that crowds the top row, and without dividers the
  three thirds (and B1's targets) are hard to see.
- Fix: draw Apple's thin dividers (hidden next to an empty slot), use `.secondary` for words, and center the
  text row in the band (move `topBand` below the text row, or split it), then re-measure in the CI shots.

**B9. One suggestion and no next-word predictions. Low.**
- Where: `ProtlyBar.swift:37-41`; `06-word-typed.png` (right slot empty after `hello`).
- User sees: the right slot is empty most of the time, never three choices.
- Why it hurts: Apple's keyboard always offers something; an empty slot looks broken. It is a scope choice, so
  low.
- Fix: when the word has no fix, show the most frequent completion from the word list; next-word prediction
  can wait.

**B10. The bar stays live while the panel is open. Medium (from code).**
- Where: `KeyboardViewController.swift:483-493` (the panel overlays only `layout.totalHeight`, the key rows) and
  `act()` lines 210-215.
- User sees: `Translate` or `Polish` stays in the center above the "Translating" spinner and above the result.
- Why it hurts: a second tap cancels the running request and starts another paid one; on the result panel it
  throws the result away and waits again. People tap again when a spinner seems slow.
- Fix: while `mode.panel != nil`, show the panel's title in the center ("Translating → English") and make the
  other slots inert, or extend the overlay over the bar.

### Protly key

**K1. One glyph for three different actions. Medium.**
- Where: `KeyboardViewController.swift:418-420, 465`; the bottom row of `01-apple.png` next to `02-first-open.png`.
- User sees: the same `character.bubble` key whether it will Translate, Polish or read the clipboard. On an
  empty field a tap brings up iOS's Allow Paste prompt.
- Why it hurts: people cannot predict the key, and a system prompt from a key that looks like the emoji key is a
  surprise. The glyph also does not match the app icon (a "p").
- Fix: change the glyph with the state (the same symbols as B2), and give the key a long-press menu with all
  three actions and the target language. Keep the system globe for switching keyboards, as the HIG asks.

**K2. VoiceOver labels are wrong or missing. Medium.**
- Where: `tree-06-word-typed.txt:137-138` (protly key read as "Keyboard Type - emojis", its image as
  "character.bubble"), line 40 (the left slot read as the quoted jamo `“ㅗ디ㅣㅐ”`), line 140 (space read as
  "space"); `Panel.swift` has no labels, traits or announcements.
- User sees (hears): the protly key announced as an emoji switch, the flip slot as a run of jamo with no verb,
  actions with no hint, and no announcement when a result arrives.
- Why it hurts: a VoiceOver user cannot find or use any Protly feature.
- Fix: `accessibilityLabel` and `accessibilityHint` on every slot ("Switch word to Korean, ㅗ디ㅣㅐ"; "Polish
  draft, replaces the text after review"), a label for the protly key that names the current action, and
  `UIAccessibility.post(notification: .screenChanged, argument:)` when the panel shows a result or an error.
  Label the result's second block (P2).

**K3. Emoji only through the globe. Low.**
- Where: `KeyboardViewController.swift:418-420`; `ProtlyApp.swift:33` ("3. Keep the Emoji keyboard").
- User sees: no emoji key; the app says to keep the Emoji keyboard but not why.
- Why it hurts: fine with Protly plus Emoji (the globe goes there), confusing with a third keyboard installed,
  where the globe cycles. It follows the HIG's "avoid duplicating system-provided keyboard features".
- Fix: one sentence in the app: "Protly's key replaces the emoji key. Use the globe for emoji."

### Panel: open, wait, result, insert

**P1. The keys disappear for the whole wait. Medium (from code).**
- Where: `KeyboardViewController.swift:225, 271`; `Panel.swift:24-28`.
- User sees: the key area turns into a card with a spinner and "Translating" until the reply comes; Cancel is the
  only control.
- Why it hurts: about 1.5 s on a good connection (Sprint 2 measurement), much longer on a weak one, with no way to
  keep typing or fix a word. Gboard's translate mode keeps the keys up; Grammarly shows its work in the strip.
- Fix: show progress in the bar (the center slot becomes a spinner with "Polishing…", the keys stay), and open
  the panel only when the result is ready. Cancel moves into the bar.

**P2. The result does not say what is on screen or what Insert will do. Medium (from code).**
- Where: `Panel.swift:29-39`; `keyboard-prompt.txt` lines 12 and 24 (the second text is a Korean back-translation
  for Translate and a Korean change list for Polish).
- User sees: two unlabeled blocks of text, then `Insert`.
- Why it hurts: the second block is the user's check on the meaning, but nothing says so; for Polish there is no
  before and after. "Insert" suggests adding text, but it replaces the whole draft (or the selection).
- Fix: a small caption over each block ("English", "뜻 확인" / "Changes"), highlight changed words for Polish,
  and rename the button "Replace" (Apple's Writing Tools uses Replace). Undo in the bar already covers the
  mistake case: keep it.

**P3. A Read clipboard result can only be closed. Medium (from code).**
- Where: `Panel.swift:39` (`mode == .in ? nil`, close label "Done").
- User sees: the Korean translation of a copied message, and Done.
- Why it hurts: people often want to quote or keep the translation; they have to retype it.
- Fix: add Copy (writing the pasteboard is allowed with Full Access) and, when the field is empty, Insert.

**P4. Errors always offer Retry, and some read like build notes. Medium (from code).**
- Where: `Panel.swift:40-44`; `KeyboardViewController.swift:217, 252-253`; `ActionAPI.swift:30-43`.
- User sees: "API key rejected. Rebuild protly with a valid key.", "protly was built without an API key. Run
  make-secrets.sh and rebuild.", "Turn on Full Access in Settings to use protly actions", "Copy a message first.",
  each with Retry.
- Why it hurts: Retry cannot help for Full Access, a missing or rejected key, a refusal, a too-long text, an empty
  clipboard or a denied paste, so the button teaches people that Retry is useless. Build instructions mean nothing
  to a user.
- Fix: mark each `ActionError.Kind` as retryable or not; show Retry only for network, timeout, rate, overloaded,
  server and parse. For the rest show Done and plain words ("Protly is not set up. Open the Protly app."). Keep
  the developer detail in the log.

**P5. Long waits have no feedback, and the wait can be 90 s. Medium (from code).**
- Where: `ActionAPI.swift:101-102` (idle 30 s, total 90 s); `Panel.swift:24-28`.
- User sees: the same spinner the whole time.
- Why it hurts: on a weak signal people cannot tell slow from stuck; they cancel and retry (another paid call) or
  leave the keyboard.
- Fix: after about 4 s change the text to "Still working… (weak connection?)"; stream the reply (Sprint 2 already
  recommended it) so text appears early; consider a shorter idle timeout (15 s) for the keyboard, where people
  are waiting with a draft in hand. Offline fails fast with "Network error. Check the connection and try again.",
  which is fine.

**P6. Tapping an action moves the cursor and does not put it back. Medium (from code).**
- Where: `FieldText.swift:11-13` (moves the cursor to the end to read the whole field);
  `KeyboardViewController.swift:238-240` ("Could not read the whole text. Select all, then tap again.").
- User sees: the cursor jumps to the end of the draft as soon as Translate or Polish is tapped, and stays there
  after Cancel or an error.
- Why it hurts: someone editing in the middle of a message loses their place even when they change their mind.
  The "Select all" error asks for a two-step workaround the user cannot see the reason for.
- Fix: remember how far the cursor moved and move it back on Cancel and on every error. For the partial read,
  offer "Use the text I can see" (the read prefix) as well as the select-all hint.

**P7. Fixed font sizes ignore Dynamic Type. Low (from code).**
- Where: `Panel.swift:32, 34, 47, 81` (`.system(size: 17)` and `15`); `ProtlyBar.swift:23-24` (one line in a
  fixed 29 pt row).
- User sees: panel text at one size whatever the system text size; at large sizes the bar text clips.
- Why it hurts: the HIG asks for text that can grow to 200 percent; the panel is where people read a full
  translation. Apple's own keys do not scale, so the keycaps can stay as they are.
- Fix: `.font(.body)` and `.font(.subheadline)` in the panel (it already scrolls), and a
  `.dynamicTypeSize(...DynamicTypeSize.xxLarge)` cap on the bar so it scales a little without breaking.

**P8. Panel buttons are bare text near the bottom edge. Low (from code).**
- Where: `Panel.swift:73-82`.
- User sees: "Cancel" and "Insert" as plain words, about 22 pt tall.
- Why it hurts: small targets under the 44 pt default, right above the globe and dictation keys.
- Fix: `.buttonStyle(.bordered)` for Cancel and `.borderedProminent` for the primary, with
  `.frame(minHeight: 44)`.

**P9. The panel card does not match the keyboard's glass, and light mode is untested. Low (from code).**
- Where: `Panel.swift:59` (`secondarySystemBackground`, 6 pt inset, 12 pt corners).
- User sees (dark, from code): an opaque gray card on top of iOS 26's glass keyboard.
- Why it hurts: it looks like a sheet from another app; in light mode nobody has looked at it.
- Fix: use the key background color or a material that matches the keyboard, and add light mode plus one panel
  scenario per state (loading, result, error, About me) to the keyboard-shots workflow with a stubbed
  `ActionClient`, so the panel gets the same visual check as the keys.

**P10. "The text changed" throws the result away. Low (from code).**
- Where: `KeyboardViewController.swift:301-303`.
- User sees: after any edit during the wait, Insert shows "The text changed. Try again." and Retry makes a new
  call.
- Why it hurts: the result was good; only the replace was unsafe.
- Fix: keep the result on screen with Copy (P3) next to Retry.

### Full Access and onboarding

**F1. Full Access is found missing only after a tap. High.**
- Where: `KeyboardViewController.swift:216-218`; `ProtlyBar.swift:45-51` shows actions regardless.
- User sees: `Translate` in the bar; tap; the keys are replaced by "Turn on Full Access in Settings to use protly
  actions" and a Retry that does nothing.
- Why it hurts: every action is dead without Full Access, and the bar promises them anyway. It is the first thing
  a new user meets.
- Fix: when `hasFullAccess` is false, draw the action dimmed with a lock symbol; on tap show the exact path
  ("Settings > General > Keyboard > Keyboards > Protly > Allow Full Access") with Done only (P4). Typing, flip
  and suggestions keep working without Full Access, which is right.

**F2. The app's setup is three lines of text with no privacy note. High.**
- Where: `ProtlyApp.swift:30-34`.
- User sees: "1. Settings > General > Keyboard > Keyboards > Add New Keyboard > Protly", "2. Tap Protly and turn on
  Allow Full Access", "3. Keep the Emoji keyboard". No button, no status, no reason.
- Why it hurts: iOS warns that Full Access lets the developer transmit what you type. Nothing in Protly says what
  it actually sends (the field text or the clipboard text and the About me note, to the model API, only when an
  action is tapped; never keystrokes), so a careful user refuses and a trusting one grants without knowing. App
  Review's keyboard rules (guideline 4.4.1) also expect the app to explain this.
- Fix: a first section "Turn on Protly" with an "Open Settings" button (`UIApplication.openSettingsURLString`
  opens Protly's own Settings page, which lists its keyboard), the steps with short reasons, and a "What Protly
  sends" paragraph. The app cannot see whether Full Access is on (no App Group); the keyboard can, so F1's
  in-keyboard state covers that side.

### Container app

**A1. No tutorial for the keyboard's features. Medium.**
- Where: `ProtlyApp.swift` (whole file).
- User sees: Setup, About me, Test. Nothing about the flip slot, the center actions, the `ru:` / `ja:` / `ko:`
  prefixes, Undo, or the protly key.
- Why it hurts: the HIG asks for exactly this in the app, and these features cannot be discovered by looking at the
  keyboard (the prefixes are invisible).
- Fix: a "How to use" section of five one-line items, each with a sample to try in the Test field ("Type
  `dkssud` and tap the left slot", "Type `ru: 고마워` and a space, then Translate").

**A2. Updating About me is a six-step clipboard dance. Medium.**
- Where: `ProtlyApp.swift:45-54` (the footer spells out the steps); `KeyboardViewController.swift:256-267`.
- User sees: a long footer: copy, tap the Test field, tap Read clipboard, Allow Paste, Save.
- Why it hurts: the steps are needed (no App Group), but a footer is the least read text on the screen, and the
  Copy button turns into "Copied" with no next step.
- Fix: after Copy, focus the Test field (bring the keyboard up) and show a short numbered banner above it. In the
  keyboard, when the marker is found, the confirm panel already explains the rest.

**A3. No settings at all. Low.**
- Where: `KeyboardViewController.swift:61-66` (haptics and auto-capitalization forced off).
- User sees: no switch for haptics, auto-capitalization, or the default target language anywhere.
- Why it hurts: right for the current owner, wrong for anyone else; Apple's keyboard lets people turn on haptic
  feedback, and Gboard has both sound and haptic toggles. A container switch cannot reach the keyboard without an
  App Group.
- Fix: a small settings panel inside the keyboard (from the protly key's long-press, K1) that writes the
  keyboard's own `UserDefaults`; the app's tutorial says where it is.

**A4. The Test field sits last. Low.**
- Where: `ProtlyApp.swift:55-57`.
- User sees: the place to try the keyboard below a long About me form.
- Why it hurts: the first thing to do after setup is to try the keyboard, and it is the last thing on the
  screen.
- Fix: move Test to just below Setup.

### Key feedback

**H1. Key feedback is thinner than Apple's. Low.**
- Where: `KeyboardViewController.swift:61-64` (haptics off); `Vendor/KeyboardKit/VENDORED.md:20` (input callouts,
  the key pop-ups, off).
- User sees: no pop-up over a pressed key and no haptic, only the key's press color and the click sound
  (KeyboardKit's audio feedback is on by default, as the HIG asks).
- Why it hurts: pop-ups are how people confirm the letter on small keys; with neither pop-up nor haptic, a mistyped
  letter is only seen in the field. The pop-ups were removed because row 1's were clipped at the keyboard's top
  edge.
- Fix: keep pop-ups on rows 2 and 3 and shorten row 1's to fit (the same approach the action-callout patch
  already takes), or make them a setting (A3). Verify on the phone that the click sound plays with Full Access on
  and off.

## The 5 changes that would most improve daily use

1. **Make every bar slot a full-size button (B1), with B8's dividers.** One layout change in `ProtlyBar.swift`
   turns three 20 pt targets into 130 x 46 pt ones. It affects every flip, every suggestion and every action,
   many times a message.
2. **Make actions look like actions and say where they go (B2, B5, K1).** A symbol, the accent tint and the target
   ("→ English", "→ Русский", "Polish 한") in the center slot and on the protly key. This removes accidental paid
   calls and confirms the prefix was understood before the wait.
3. **Keep the action steady and the left slot quiet (B3, B4).** Show the action while a word is typed, and dim the
   flip reading unless it is plausible. Together these stop the bar from changing on every key, so the one change
   that matters (a word in the wrong language) stands out.
4. **Keep the keys up while waiting, and give the result clear words (P1, P2, P4, P5, P6).** Progress in the bar,
   the panel only for the result, labeled blocks, "Replace", Retry only when it can work, a "still working" line,
   and the cursor restored on Cancel.
5. **Fix the first run (F1, F2, A1).** An "Open Settings" button, a plain "what Protly sends" note, a five-line
   tutorial in the app, and a dimmed, locked action in the keyboard when Full Access is off. A new user gets to a
   working Translate on the first try instead of an error panel.

Close behind: K2 (VoiceOver labels, small code, large gain for anyone using it), B7 (late fixes), and a CI panel
scenario plus light mode (P9) so the panel gets the screenshot check the keys already have.
